## **Overview: A Structural Reset with Stronger Economics**

FY 2025-26 was a year of significant transition for Ola Electric Mobility Limited. The Company undertook a comprehensive reset of its operating model to address service execution, distribution productivity, operating expenditure, inventory and working-capital management. These actions were intended to establish a leaner and more accountable operating structure capable of supporting growth with improved unit economics and greater capital discipline.

Deliveries and revenue contracted materially during the year; however, the underlying economics of the business strengthened. Consolidated gross margin increased from 17.9% in FY25 to 30.6% in FY26, while gross profit declined by only 14.4% despite a 50.1% reduction in revenue. Consolidated operating expenditure declined by 33.9%, the adjusted operating EBITDA loss narrowed by 36.8%, and operating cash outflow reduced by 67.6%. In Q4 FY26, consolidated gross margin reached 38.5% and the Company recorded positive consolidated cash flow from operations of ₹91 crore.

The Company’s priority is now to convert this stronger operating base into sustained demand, higher asset utilisation and improved cash generation. In Automotive, the focus is on maintaining service consistency, strengthening the product portfolio, developing productive distribution and rebuilding volumes without recreating the earlier cost structure. In Cells, the focus is on commissioning and utilising the 6 GWh platform, improving manufacturing yield and quality, increasing own-Cell integration and converting identified demand opportunities into qualified commercial sales.

### **Economic and Industry Context**

#### Indian economy

India's economy remained resilient during FY 2025-26. Provisional estimates released by the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation indicate real GDP growth of 7.7%, supported by growth in consumption, fixed capital formation, manufacturing and services. This macroeconomic backdrop remains supportive of long-term mobility demand, although consumer financing conditions, inflation in input costs and global geopolitical developments can affect near-term purchasing decisions and manufacturing economics.

#### Electric two-wheeler industry

The structural case for electric two-wheelers continues to be supported by lower running costs, improving product capability, increasing consumer awareness and policy support for domestic manufacturing. The competitive environment, however, intensified during FY26 as established and new manufacturers expanded their portfolios, pricing activity increased and customers placed greater emphasis on reliability, financing and access to service.

The industry's next stage is likely to be defined less by initial product availability and more by the quality of execution across the full ownership cycle. Product economics, battery performance, financing, fulfilment, service accessibility, warranty experience and resale confidence will influence adoption. For manufacturers, this requires balancing market development with margin, working capital and service readiness rather than pursuing volume in isolation.

Public policy is also shifting the industry toward deeper localisation. Demand-side support remains relevant, while production-linked incentives for Automotive components and Advanced Chemistry Cells encourage domestic technology, manufacturing and value addition. Ola's vertically integrated model is aligned with this direction, but the commercial benefits depend on reliable production, competitive cost, compliance and sustained customer demand.
