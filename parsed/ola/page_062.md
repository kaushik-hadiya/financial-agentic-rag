## **Business Overview and Operating Transition**

### **Business Overview and FY26 Operating Transition**

Ola operates across two connected businesses. The Automotive business develops, manufactures and sells electric two-wheelers and supports customers through software, service and related offerings. The Cell business develops and manufactures advanced battery Cells for captive mobility, energy-storage and selected external applications.

During FY26, the Company redesigned significant elements of its operating model following a period of rapid expansion. The reset covered service operations, retail footprint, workforce deployment, expenditure governance, inventory management, fulfilment and working capital. The objective was to remove duplication, improve accountability and establish a lower recurring cost base while preserving the capabilities required to support future growth.

#### Quarterly progression

The improvement was visible through the year, with consolidated gross margin increasing from 25.8% in Q1 FY26 to 30.9% in Q2, 34.3% in Q3 and 38.5% in Q4. Q3 represented the most intensive phase of the operating intervention, while Q4 provided evidence of improved service performance, lower operating expenditure and stronger cash-flow management.

The Q4 outcome should be viewed as an important milestone rather than a normalised run rate, as consolidated adjusted operating EBITDA and free cash flow remained negative and cash flow also benefited from PLI receipts and working-capital movements. The central test for the next phase is whether the Company can preserve service, margin and cost discipline while rebuilding volumes.

#### A leaner operating model

The retail network was rationalised to approximately 700 stores by Q3 FY26 as the Company shifted its focus from footprint size to location productivity and local relevance. Corporate and operating functions were simplified, workforce deployment was reviewed and recurring expenditure was subjected to tighter governance.

The objective of the reset was to remove duplication, manual hand-offs and fixed activities that did not create sufficient customer or financial value. Future investments will be assessed against demand visibility, service readiness, contribution, cash requirements and the expected utilisation of existing assets.
