## **Automotive Business Review**

### Gen 3 and vertical integration

The Gen 3 platform forms the foundation of the current Automotive portfolio. Its common architecture, increased use of internally developed motors and electronics, and integrated software reduce complexity across product development, manufacturing, diagnostics, service and spare-parts management. These capabilities supported the improvement in gross margin and provide a common platform for future products.

The Company will continue to internalise technologies that materially influence product performance, economics and supply-chain resilience, while working with external partners where they provide specialised capability or efficient scale. Internally developed systems will be scaled after meeting defined requirements relating to safety, quality, reliability, yield and cost.

### Service stabilisation and product quality

Service recovery was a key customer-facing priority during FY26. Improved parts availability, higher technician productivity, stronger repair capability, process automation and tighter operating governance reduced average service turnaround time from approximately nine days in October 2025 to nearly one day in March 2026. Service backlog reduced from 14 days to six days and same-day closures improved to approximately 87%.

Importantly, the improvement in service performance coincided with a recovery in registrations. As service turnaround time, backlog and parts availability improved, registrations increased to 12,166 units in April 2026, up 20% month-on-month, even as the broader electric two-wheeler market declined by more than 22%. This momentum continued into Q1 FY27, when registrations increased by 97% quarter-on-quarter and market share improved from 5.1% in Q4 FY26 to 8.4%. While registrations are also influenced by products, pricing, financing, distribution and broader market conditions, the parallel improvement indicates that restoring service reliability was an important prerequisite for rebuilding customer trust and demand.

The next priority is to maintain service consistency as volumes recover. Distribution expansion, parts stocking and service capacity will be planned together, while field-service data will continue to inform engineering, manufacturing and product-quality improvements.
