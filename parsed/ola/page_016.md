## **Control the technology. Control the outcome.**

Vertical integration has always been central to Ola Electric's strategy. FY26 showed why. When supply-chain risks, cost pressure or product requirements changed, in-house engineering created options that an assembly-led model would not have.

### Rare-earth-free motor: resilience through engineering.

As rare-earth supply risk increased, the company accelerated an in-house ferrite motor programme. The objective was not merely substitution; it was continuity, cost control and performance parity without depending on scarce magnetic materials.

### In-house ABS and electronics: regulation becomes an engineering opportunity.

The ABS transition in two-wheelers created another example of technology ownership turning a regulatory requirement into a potential cost and differentiation advantage. The same logic applies across motors, controllers, power electronics, braking and software.

### AI became an operating layer, not a demo.

By Q4, Ola's in-house AI stack was operating at production scale across sales, service and operations, handling around two lakh connected calls per day. AI agents supported lead reactivation, multilingual engagement, test-ride scheduling, technician productivity, registration, logistics and network governance.

### Software + hardware + data create a faster feedback loop.

Owning the connected vehicle, software stack, service data and manufacturing processes allows insight to travel faster through the company: a problem can be diagnosed in service, engineered in R&D, validated in software or hardware and fed back into manufacturing with fewer hand-offs.
